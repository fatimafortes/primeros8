## 1. My angle

My job is to bring the kill, with a source. I tested eleven kills
against the model: three died, most came out wounded, and three survived
primary sources. Those three are the ones I carry into the session:

- **Kill 4b: the vendor excludes the hero.** Meta's own rules bar
  children under 10 from Quest 2 and 3, and ages 10–12 need an account
  created and managed by a parent. "Honest VR rehearsal for one school"
  cannot reach most of primary school, even though the Mexican 19S app
  markets itself as ideal for schools.

- **Kill T: the drill is alert-first; 19S was shake-first.** This
  Saturday's national drill (Sept 19, 2026) uses an inland 7.7 in
  Tehuacán, yet it still starts with the C5 loudspeakers and a phone
  alert. In 2017, CIRES's own Sept 28 report walked back the 20 seconds
  of warning first announced: people in CDMX felt the quake before the
  alert sounded.

- **Kill 2d: the standard pays for hours, not performance.** CDMX's
  brigade standard (NT-SGIRPC-CAP-006-3-2024) defines in-person courses
  by hours and topics (evacuation: 3, 3 and 4 hours) and records
  evacuation times. Nobody is required to buy proof that people act
  correctly, so nobody does.

What the other roles won't see: the incumbent is not standing still. The
drill is free, now falls on a Saturday to reach families, uses regional
scenarios, pushes phone alerts and hands out a free participation
certificate.

> **Numbers that moved (for the Fusion).** The 8.1M participants and
> 4,500 schools are Mexico City's figures for the Sept 2025 drill;
> nationally, 29,389 schools took part. The 4.5% insured figure dates
> from 2019. In Sept 2025 AMIS reported 25.8% of homes with any
> insurance, mostly tied to mortgages, while owner-chosen coverage
> stayed around 6.5–6.7% from 2019 to 2024, and agents still say only
> about 5 in 100 homes carry a quake policy. Quest 3/3S went on sale in
> Mexico in May 2025. "Same scenario every year" is outdated: 2018 used
> an inland Puebla scenario and 2026 uses five regional ones.

## 2. The technology, honestly

**Commodity:** standalone headsets at MX\$6,600, 3D disaster scenes, and
in-app performance scoring (MetaPlay, a Mexican 19S app, already ships
statistics). VR brigade simulators are already sold by Save.mx, which
describes itself as an accredited third party in 28 states. **Hard:**
proving that behavior in VR transfers to a real quake. The strongest
study I found (Indonesia, JMIR 2025) beat standard training on knowledge
and skills assessments after 3 months, but with 400 adult volunteers,
excluding anyone prone to motion sickness, and never in a real quake.
Auckland measured only knowledge and self-efficacy, and 6 of 93
participants quit from motion sickness. Also hard: running a fleet on
consumer headsets from a vendor that stopped selling business headsets
and managed services in February 2026, with support only until January
2030. **Fake:** "lived experience" claims with nothing measured, and app
"certificates" when the state gives one away. And a phone cannot shake a
room: any phone trigger is a signal, which is alert-first again.

## 3. The world's best attempt (global → local)

- **Japan's disaster-prevention centers** (Tokyo Fire Department,
  Ikebukuro, since 1986). Free guided tours with a room that shakes at
  intensity 7, the top of Japan's scale. **Right:** physical, public,
  repeatable. **Why not Mexico:** budget and scale, not the idea; CDMX's
  version is a bus built for children, with a 1.5 × 1 m platform for two
  people at a time. **What breaks:** a startup would compete with
  something the state gives away, and I found no evaluation of behavior
  change.

- **The Great ShakeOut** (Southern California, 2008; more than 56
  million participants worldwide in 2023). A one-minute drop, cover and
  hold on drill, free and without a headset. **Right:** it drills the
  no-warning action at scale. **Why not Mexico:** the mass-drill slot
  already belongs to the state, which teaches the same action. **What
  breaks:** it adds nothing here, but it proves the shake-first action
  doesn't need VR.

- **Enterprise VR training.** The survivor: Strivr, whose modules run on
  about 17,000 headsets across Walmart's US stores; Walmart reported
  10–15% higher test scores, which again measures knowledge. The dead:
  Engage XR, which announced a court-approved liquidation this month
  after its largest customer stopped taking licenses, leaving users
  unsure how to get their content out. **Right:** real enterprise
  adoption. **What breaks:** smaller Mexican budgets, dependence on one
  client, and, in a compliance product, inspection records that die with
  the vendor.

**Translation:** the best global answers are free, public and physical.
Whatever crosses the border has to beat free.

## 4. The vacuum I'd attack: Measurement

Through my lens, it is the only door I couldn't close.
**Compliance-upgrade** dies at Kill 2d. **Family** is being entered by
the free incumbent with the Saturday drill, even if "3 a.m., who takes
grandma" is still empty. **Insurance bridge** has no lever: premiums are
priced on zone, construction and insured value, not on how the occupants
rehearse. **Measurement** survives, but not for VR: an unannounced drill
and a stopwatch measure more than a headset does.

**My gift to the Blueprint, breaks already labeled:** an unannounced
drill, with surprise as the trigger, that measures each worker's
protective action in the first seconds, sold to multinational plants in
Mexico as a layer on top of existing mustering software. **Known
breaks:** schools are out (the SEP is preparing a national ban on phones
and other digital devices in primary and secondary schools, plus minors'
data and no budget); we risk being a feature of mustering tools; phones
may be banned on production lines; and individual timing needs genuinely
free worker consent under the 2025 data law.

## 5. The shadow

The chapter shows synthetic experience that feels like memory being used
to exploit grief. The Mexican version already exists: an app that
recreates 19S inside a building that quake damaged, offered to a city
that lived through it. And even the calm walk has a cost: CDMX's 2025
drill left one person injured and three with panic attacks. **Design
decisions:** (1) hypothetical scenarios only, never reconstructions of
real tragedies, sites or victims; (2) opt-in with a trauma pre-check, a
one-tap exit and a non-immersive option; (3) measure the action, not the
fear: no face video, no biometric or emotional capture, and employers
see aggregate results only, never individual scores used for discipline
or sales.

## 6. My opening position for Team Bending

**FALSE, on both halves.** The mass drill has proven reach, not proven
behavior change, and it rehearses alert-first while 19S was shake-first.
VR for one school is just as unproven, most primary students can't use
the headset, and a headset session is the most announced drill there is.
Both are unproven, and the burden of proof sits with whoever claims to
save lives. I will also ask the team to show that the drop in deaths
from 1985 to 2017 came from drills rather than from post-1985 building
codes. My bet is the codes.
